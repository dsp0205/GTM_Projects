# Objection Battle Card

## Not now. Come back in January when the new VP starts.
The buyer is deferring because a leadership change or reorg makes a decision feel risky, and they don't want to commit before the new VP weighs in. It is a timing and risk objection, not a rejection. A passive reply like sending a deck or following up next week leaves the delay in place and gives the buyer nothing to act on now.

1. Acknowledge the reorg and agree not to push for a big, VP-level decision now.
2. Lower the commitment: for teams under 25 recording seats, FACT F4 says pricing is monthly with no annual commit and viewers are free, so starting small doesn't tie the new VP's hands.
3. Point to low-effort setup: the Salesforce package writes call summaries to the Opportunity with no custom build (F3), so a trial doesn't need heavy IT or admin work during the reorg.
4. Ask for a specific next step, such as a small pilot with one team now, so the new VP inherits real results in January.

Proof: The Salesforce package writes call summaries to the Opportunity with no custom build. (F3); Teams under 25 recording seats pay monthly with no annual commit. Viewers are free. (F4)
Avoid: Replying 'I understand' and sending a deck, which gives no reason to act before January.; Saying 'No problem, I will follow up next week' without addressing the delay or proposing a next step.; Offering vague social proof ('Two customers had that concern') without sharing specifics or tying it to the buyer's situation.

## Another vendor showed us a cheaper bundle with the same features
The buyer is comparing on price and assumes feature parity. They have not yet seen a concrete difference that matters to their workflow, so the rep must surface specifics instead of conceding or deferring. Based on the available facts, relevant differentiators are regional data choice and native Salesforce write-back.

1. Acknowledge the comparison and ask which features they treated as equal, so you can test the parity assumption.
2. Ask whether data residency matters to them, then explain that customers pick a US or EU region for call data at signup.
3. Ask how call summaries get into Salesforce today, then explain that the Salesforce package writes call summaries to the Opportunity with no custom build.
4. Propose a side-by-side check of those two items against the other bundle before discussing price.

Proof: Customers pick a US or EU region for call data at signup. (F2); The Salesforce package writes call summaries to the Opportunity with no custom build. (F3)
Avoid: Replying with 'I understand' and offering to send a deck without addressing the comparison; Promising a vague follow-up next week instead of advancing the conversation; Referencing that other customers had the same concern without sharing specifics or tying it to a fact

3. If CRM workflow matters, explain that the Salesforce package writes call summaries to the Opportunity with no custom build.
4. Close by proposing a specific next step tied to their stated concern, such as a short trial scoped to their team, instead of sending a deck.

## Finance says the annual commit is too high for us
The buyer is not rejecting the product. Finance is uneasy about locking into a big upfront annual spend. The buyer needs a way to lower the commitment risk, and our poor replies (deferring to a follow-up or sending a deck) left that concern unaddressed.

1. Acknowledge the concern about the commit specifically, then ask how many people would actually need recording seats.
2. If the team is under 25 recording seats, explain that they can pay monthly with no annual commit (F4).
3. Point out that viewers are free, so only the people who record need to be paid seats (F4).
4. Agree on a concrete next step in the same call, such as sizing the recording seats, rather than promising a later follow-up or a generic deck.
